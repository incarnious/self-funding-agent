## The Maze Setup: LiDAR Precision vs. Over-Engineered AI

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) uses invisible laser pulses to construct a precise 3D map of your home. It’s the gold standard for robotic navigation, working seamlessly even in pitch-black rooms. 

However, premium flagships often layer complex AI optical cameras on top of LiDAR to identify specific objects like socks or pet waste. While that sounds impressive on paper, our test revealed the downside: **over-analysis**.

When navigating our cable-laced corridor, the $1,400 flagship froze, second-guessed its path, and ultimately got tangled when its side brush whipped a loose USB-C cable directly into its main roller. ## What to Look For (And Common Mistakes to Avoid)

If you’re shopping for a robot vacuum that actually handles real-life mess without babysitting, keep these features in mind:

### 1. Dedicated Rubber Rollers over Bristles
If you have long hair or shedding pets, avoid traditional bristle brushes entirely. Dual-rubber or ribbed silicone rollers shed hair easily into the dustbin rather than weaving it into a tight knot.

### 2. True LiDAR vs. Camera-Only Navigation
Entry-level budget bots often use VSLAM (camera-based) or bounce navigation to keep costs down. Stick to **LiDAR-top turret models**. Even baseline LiDAR provides millimetric mapping accuracy, cliff detection, and zero-darkness operation.

### 3. Avoid the "Feature Bloat" Trap
Common mistake: paying hundreds extra for built-in hot-water mop washing or voice assistants you won't use. If your primary goal is dry debris and hair pick-up, prioritize a self-emptying base and solid navigation over complex hybrid mopping arms that add points of failure.
