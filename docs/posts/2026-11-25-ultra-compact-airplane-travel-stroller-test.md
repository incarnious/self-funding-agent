## What to Look For: The Airport Audit Standard

Before buying into designer labels, audit prospective strollers using the same strict standards you would apply to a precision safety razor or high-performance beauty tool:

*   **True One-Handed Auto-Fold:** If a fold requires two hands, a knee, and three steps, it fails at the gate. Look for gravity-assisted mechanisms that trigger with a single hand press.
*   **IATA Overhead Bin Clearance:** Many strollers claim to be "cabin approved," but force you to pop off the rear wheels or detach the bumper bar to close the bin door. True travel gear glides into overhead storage like a sleek makeup compact into a clutch.
*   **Chassis Flex & Wheel Shock Absorption:** Smooth terminal floors hide poor suspension. Dropping off sidewalk curbs and navigating airport expansion joints reveals whether a frame absorbs impact or jars your sleeping child awake.

## Common Travel Gear Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Over-indexing on plush fabric over frame weight:** Heavy canvas and thick padding add bulk. Look for breathable, lightweight textiles that wipe clean as easily as a silicone blending sponge.
2.  **Assuming higher cost equals better travel mobility:** Premium daily strollers are built for suburban sidewalks, not tight airplane aisles or rapid security line collapses.
3.  **Ignoring the unfold process:** A stroller that folds in one second but requires a complex two-handed shake to lock open will frustrate you at baggage claim.
