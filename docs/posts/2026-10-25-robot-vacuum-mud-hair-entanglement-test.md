## What Real Life (and Bathroom Spills) Looks Like on a Floor

Standard vacuum testing relies on clean, dry rice or pet kibble. But real homes deal with much tougher messes: sticky clay face masks, damp mud tracked in from outdoors, fine bronzer powders, and long human hair wrapped around furniture legs. 

Our test simulated a week of heavy bathroom and entryway chaos. We laid down a 2.5-liter mixture of damp synthetic mud, dried charcoal face mask flakes, fine cosmetic powders, coarse men's beard stubble, and 12-inch human hair strands. 

When shopping for a vacuum to handle this specific daily debris, look for three critical features:
*   **Rubberized, V-Shaped Roller Brushes:** Traditional bristled brushes act like hair velcro, requiring you to manually slice off tangled strands with scissors every few days.
*   **Straight-Through Extraction Channels:** High-PA suction means nothing if the air path narrows sharply before reaching the dustbin, causing wet mud or hair clumps to jam.
*   **High-Pressure Dock Suction:** A self-emptying base needs enough air velocity to pull tightly wound hair off the internal roller during the auto-empty cycle.
