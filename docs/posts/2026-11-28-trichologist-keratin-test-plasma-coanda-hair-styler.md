## The Science: Thermal Degradation vs. High-Velocity Airflow

Hair is primarily made of keratin, a dense structural protein bound by hydrogen and disulfide links. When traditional hot tools reach temperatures approaching 450°F, keratin undergoes irreversible denaturation. Internal moisture rapidly vaporizes, creating tiny microscopic bubbles inside the hair shaft—a condition dermatologists call "bubble hair."

To protect your cuticle, modern scalp and hair experts evaluate appliances on a simple ratio: airflow velocity versus heat output. 

*   **Thermal Styling:** Relies on high heat to force hair into shape, melting the cuticle layer over time.
*   **Coanda Airflow Styling:** Utilizes high-velocity air streams that naturally wrap hair around a barrel using negative pressure, setting curls and sleek styles without cooking the fiber.

When high-velocity airflow is paired with plasma technology—which balances positive and negative ions—the dryer seals the hair cuticle, traps moisture inside, and eliminates static electricity. High airspeed does the heavy lifting so high heat doesn't have to.

## Common Mistakes That Cause Heat Damage

Even the most advanced tool can damage your hair if used incorrectly. Avoid these frequent styling errors:

*   **Prioritizing Heat Over Airspeed:** Using maximum heat with low fan speed cooks the hair surface. Always crank the airflow to maximum and keep the heat setting on medium or low.
*   **Styling Soaking-Wet Strands:** Hydrogen bonds are weakest when hair is wet. Towel-dry or rough-dry your hair to about 80% dry before applying styling attachments or tension.
*   **Skipping the Cool Shot:** Heat opens the cuticle and makes hair pliable; cold air seals the cuticle and freezes the style in place. End every section with a 5-second cold burst.
