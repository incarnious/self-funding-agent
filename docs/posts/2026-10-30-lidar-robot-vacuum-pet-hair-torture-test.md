## Key Features to Look For (And What to Skip)

When shopping across tiers—whether budget ($150–$250), mid-range ($400–$700), or premium ($1,000+)—focus on the mechanics that actually impact daily cleaning rather than flashy gimmicks:

*   **LiDAR + AI Obstacle Avoidance:** Traditional bumper-navigation is like applying foundation in the dark. Look for top-mounted LiDAR turrets paired with front-facing camera AI to map rooms in seconds and dodge low-profile hazards like socks or pet waste.
*   **Rubber Brushes vs. Bristles:** Hair wraps around traditional bristled brushes like wet mascara on an eyelash wand. Opt for solid rubber, treaded rollers or floating brush guards that route long strands directly into the dust intake.
*   **Self-Empty Dock Suction:** A self-empty base needs short, direct air paths to pull dense hair mats out of the dustbin without clogging the dock filter.

## Common Mistakes Robot Vacuum Buyers Make

*   **Chasing Pascals (Pa) Instead of Airflow:** High suction numbers look great on paper, but if the brush roll design is poor, high Pa just binds hair tighter around the roller. Think of it like skincare: active ingredients only work if the delivery system is right.
*   **Ignoring Base Station Maintenance:** Even self-emptying docks need monthly upkeep. Failing to clear hair off the roller axles or clean the optical sensors leads to navigation drift and false "dustbin full" errors.
*   **Assuming Expensive Means Tangle-Free:** High-end models often pack extra features like heated mop drying and automatic water refills, but their core sweeping mechanisms can still get defeated by a single loose phone cord.
