## Why Most Night Vision Fails (The "White-Cast" Problem)

If you have ever applied a heavy mineral sunscreen or used the wrong shade of concealer, you know the dreaded "white-cast"—that ghostly, blown-out look under direct light. Traditional infrared (IR) night vision suffers from the exact same flaw.

When a camera blasts cheap IR light at an approaching subject, it washes out skin tones, turns facial features into smooth white blobs, and loses fine line detail entirely. Worse, to compensate for pitch-black environments, camera firmware often lowers the shutter speed. The result? A crystal-clear picture of a empty room, but a blurry, unusable smudge the second a person walks through the frame.

---

## Key Features That Actually Matter

To get true detail in dark conditions, ignore raw megapixel counts and focus on these three core specifications:

*   **Aperture Size (f/1.6 or lower):** Think of the aperture like the iris of an eye. A lower f-number lets significantly more ambient light hit the sensor, reducing the need for harsh artificial illumination.
*   **Sensor Size over 4K Resolution:** A 4K label on a tiny sensor is like wearing heavy foundation over dry skin—it highlights imperfections instead of fixing them. Larger image sensors capture more photons per pixel, keeping skin texture and clothing sharp.
*   **On-Device AI Shutter Speed Control:** Advanced algorithms instantly recognize a human shape and force the camera to maintain a high shutter speed, freezing motion without blurring the face.

## Common Night Vision Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Mounting Behind Glass:** IR light bounces off window glass like a flash mirror, leaving you with an overexposed glow and zero visibility outside. Always install night-vision cameras outdoors or use glass-compatible ambient light setups.
2.  **Relying Solely on High Megapixels:** High resolution without low-light sensitivity creates noisy, grainy footage in the dark.
3.  **Ignoring Camera Height:** Placing cameras too high captures the tops of hats and foreheads rather than clear eye-level detail. Mount units between 7 and 9 feet for optimal coverage.
